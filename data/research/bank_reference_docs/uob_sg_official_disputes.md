# UOB Singapore: Card disputes and questionnaire reference

Source institution: United Overseas Bank (UOB), Singapore. Source pages checked 2026-11-15. This is a concise, original-language research summary of UOB's official materials, stored only in the external `bank_pdf_docs` collection. It is not Lion City Bank policy.

## Distinguish fraud, scams, lost cards, and merchant disputes

UOB's current card-dispute guidance separates a scam (the customer was manipulated into an action), lost or stolen card use, unauthorized card fraud, and a merchant dispute (the customer made the payment but has an issue such as non-delivery, defective goods, duplicate or incorrect billing, or a missing refund). Before raising a case, it recommends checking merchant descriptors, posting dates, and whether a family member or friend may have used the card. An unfamiliar merchant name alone does not prove fraud.

UOB's published process routes suspected scams, lost/stolen cards, and fraud through UOB TMRW's reporting and card-blocking flow; merchant disputes use a separate dispute flow. Its page says an authorized transaction generally cannot simply be stopped after authorization, and pending transactions may need to post before a chargeback review can begin. These are UOB-specific process details; recheck the official page before presenting current instructions.

Official source: https://www.uob.com.sg/personal/cards/services/transaction-dispute.page

## UOB case questionnaires

UOB publishes separate scam and lost/stolen-card questionnaires. The scam questionnaire asks about credential sharing, impersonation, remote-access software, the communication channel, the loss amount, and police-report status. The lost/stolen questionnaire asks whether the card is still held, when and how it went missing, and who may have had access. Both are case-review forms, not a promise that a dispute will be accepted or refunded.

The forms request only the last four card digits in the customer-information section. For demonstrations, use synthetic details and never collect a real full card number, PIN, password, or OTP.

Official sources:
- Scam questionnaire: https://www.uob.com.sg/assets/web-resources/personal/pdf/cards/services/transaction-dispute/scam-questionnaire.pdf
- Lost/stolen questionnaire: https://www.uob.com.sg/assets/web-resources/personal/pdf/cards/services/transaction-dispute/fraud-lost-stolen-questionnaire.pdf

## Time limits and outcomes are UOB-specific

The official page currently describes prompt reporting, a 30-calendar-day reporting instruction for unauthorized transactions, and card-scheme dispute windows that can vary by dispute reason. It also says review is case-specific and recovery is not guaranteed. Because time limits and channel instructions can change, treat them as a dated UOB snapshot, not general banking rules and not another bank's customer advice.

Official source: https://www.uob.com.sg/personal/cards/services/transaction-dispute.page
