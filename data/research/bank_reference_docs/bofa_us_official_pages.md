# Bank of America (United States): Fees and suspicious-activity reference

Source institution: Bank of America, N.A., United States. Official sources checked 2026-11-15. This summary and the source PDF are for comparative research only; U.S. account terms, dollar fees, and contact channels do not apply to Singapore banking or Lion City Bank.

## Personal Schedule of Fees

The official Personal Schedule of Fees PDF available at review time is effective November 13, 2026 and applies in all states. It groups fees by personal account and service, explains conditions and possible waivers, and notes that other account agreements may also apply. It is a useful example of how to organize fee explanations, but every fee is product- and jurisdiction-specific and can change.

Official PDF: https://www.bankofamerica.com/deposits/resources/personal-schedule-fees.go

## Reporting suspicious activity

Bank of America's official suspicious-activity guidance says to contact the number on the card or bank statement if the customer responded to a suspicious call, text, or email. It describes locking a card as one protective step, while also explaining that physical and virtual cards may need to be locked separately and that locking is not a replacement for reporting a lost or stolen card. For actual use, customers should follow the current official channel for their specific product.

Official page: https://web.bankofamerica.com/en/security/report-suspicious-activity

## Scope and use boundary

These materials cover U.S. consumer products and U.S. rules. Do not reuse dollar fees, phone numbers, fraud liability rules, statutory time limits, or product terms for a Singapore customer. At most, use the fee schedule as a document-design reference and the security page as a source for topics to independently validate against Singapore sources.
